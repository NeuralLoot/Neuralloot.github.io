What's real? Anthropic paper vs toy parts

The paper. Anthropic (Zhou & Freeman, Oct 12 2026) asks whether small weight-based models could self-replicate on commercial IoT — smart TV boxes and similar — without a datacenter. A model too big for one device can be split into shards that communicate (pipeline parallel). Their physical demo ran Gemma4-26B-A4B across 15 ~$55 Android TV boxes. Individual steps of replication are already within reach of small models; composing them end to end is where most slip. This lab is a safety-literacy game: it shows the mechanism and the risk, not how to build a worm.

How the game maps to it. Level 1 is the memory ceiling: at 4GB RAM and 4-bit weights, a 9B needs 4+ boxes and a 26B needs 10+. Level 2 is composition: many models clear individual steps (a 9B got inference up on 8 of 9 hardware types), but sub-30B models slip when chaining find → access → payload → inference → spawn. Level 3 is the floor: in their simplified evals only GLM-5.2 (and stronger) finished end-to-end replication. The hold-to-propagate crawl, the OOM flash, the clock, and the stars are game-only.

What we do not show. No exploit steps, payloads, package recipes, or device credentials. Cyber stages in the paper were largely out of scope or simplified (unauthenticated ADB in a firewalled VPC). Treat this as a threat-model sketch, not an ops guide.
