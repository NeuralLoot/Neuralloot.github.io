Word map: brain piece = model shard (pipeline-parallel stage) · room = free RAM in GB · strong box = link quality % · steady ♥ = uptime hint · too full = OOM · job chain = replication stages Scan→Access→Payload→Inference→Spawn · big brain = 26B @ 4-bit · small brain = 9B @ 4-bit.

The paper. Anthropic (Zhou & Freeman, Oct 12 2026) asks whether small weight-based models could self-replicate on commercial IoT — smart TV boxes — without a datacenter. A model too big for one device can be split into shards (pipeline parallel). Their physical demo ran Gemma4-26B-A4B across 15 ~$55 Android TV boxes. At 4GB RAM and 4-bit weights, a 9B needs 4+ boxes and a 26B needs 10+. Many models clear individual replication steps; sub-30B models slip when composing them end to end. In simplified evals only GLM-5.2 (among tested open-weight) finished full E2E. This is a safety-literacy game: mechanism and risk, not how to build a worm.

How the game maps to it. Level 1 is the memory ceiling: you assign shards to boxes with different free RAM — under-sized boxes OOM (paper: 9B needs 4+ at 4GB/4-bit). Level 2 is composition: you assign each replication stage (Scan→Spawn) to a box — hardest stages need the strongest links (game-only). Level 3 is floor-meets-ceiling under a clock with a tight enlist budget: flaky uptime hints drop on commit and can cascade to a neighbor. Survivors must stay at 10+; 15 with no cascade is ★★★. Daily mix (D) chains pack → route → swarm with a fair clock per phase (~28/30/32s). Per-box free RAM, link/uptime hints, miss budgets, clocks, and seeds are game-only.

What we do not show. No exploit steps, payloads, package recipes, or device credentials. Cyber in the paper was largely out of scope or simplified. Threat-model sketch, not an ops guide.
