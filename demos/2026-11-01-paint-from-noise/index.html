How it works

An image generator starts from pure static. Each step, the model guesses what clean picture is hiding in the static and removes a bit of noise.

Guidance: each step it guesses twice, with your prompt and without. Then it pushes further toward the "with prompt" guess. Too little: it ignores you. Too much: colors blow out.

Steps: more, smaller steps make a cleaner picture, up to a point.

How does an AI paint a picture from static?

It removes noise, step by step. You control one dial. Too low is mush. Too high burns.

What you just saw

AI image generators like Stable Diffusion are diffusion models. They learn to remove noise from pictures. To make a new picture, they start from pure static and remove a little noise at a time. Each step, the model guesses what the finished picture looks like, then moves the static a bit toward that guess.

Guidance is the dial that makes it listen to your prompt. Each step the model makes two guesses: one with your prompt and one without. Then it pushes past the "with prompt" guess, away from the "without" one. A small push and the prompt barely matters. A big push and the colors overshoot what real pixels can be, so the picture comes out harsh and burnt.

Steps are how many jumps it takes from static to picture. A few huge jumps leave a rough, smeared result. More steps help, but past a few dozen you mostly wait longer.

🤓 Nerd notes

Sampler. The game runs real DDIM updates (eta = 0) on the DDPM linear schedule: 1,000 noise levels, beta from 0.0001 to 0.02. Your step count picks evenly spaced levels from 999 down to 0.

Guidance. Each step computes eps = eps_uncond + s × (eps_cond − eps_uncond), the classifier-free guidance rule in Stable Diffusion's pipeline. In the CFG paper's notation that is w = s − 1. We clip the model's clean-picture guess to the pixel range, like Imagen's "static thresholding". Even so, high guidance stays harsh, as Imagen also reports.

The toy model. No neural network. The "model" knows 15 emoji pictures (5 kinds × 3). Its guess is a soft blend of them, weighted by how well each matches the noisy image. The no-prompt guess is kept blurrier, and the prompt guess only half-listens to the prompt, like a small, weak model. That is why guidance helps here.

Numbers differ. In our toy the sweet spot is about 1.5 to 2. Stable Diffusion's default is 7.5. Different model, different scale. The shape is the same: too low ignores the prompt, too high burns.

Judging. We compare your final picture with the 15 it knows. Nearest one is the wrong kind: off prompt. Too far from all of them: mush (flatter) or burnt (harsher). Random static is fresh every paint, so the same setting can win or lose.

Sources

Ho, Jain, Abbeel, Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (2020): T = 1,000 steps, linear betas 1e-4 to 0.02.

Song, Meng, Ermon, Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (2020): quality close to 1,000-step models in 20 to 100 steps, 10× to 50× faster.

Ho, Salimans, Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance (2022): more guidance trades variety for fidelity; "strongly guided samples" show "saturated colors".

Saharia et al., Imagen (2022): large guidance weights give "highly saturated and unnatural images".

Hugging Face Diffusers, Stable Diffusion text-to-image pipeline: default 50 steps, guidance_scale 7.5.
