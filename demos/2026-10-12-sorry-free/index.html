    What's real? Paper vs toy parts

    The paper. Google DeepMind's AlphaProof Nexus (Tsoukalas et al., "Advancing Mathematics Research with AI-Driven Formal Proof Search", Science, 2026; preprint arXiv 2605.22763) pairs language models with the Lean proof assistant. A model proposes proof steps, Lean checks every tactic, and a result only counts if the final proof is sorry-free. sorry is Lean's placeholder for "trust me, I'll prove this later".

    What it found. The full-featured agent solved 9 of 353 open Erdős problems, including two open for 56 years, and proved 44 of 492 open OEIS conjectures, at an inference cost of a few hundred dollars per problem. A basic agent (LLM generate, Lean verify, repeat; no evolution, no AlphaProof) also solved all 9 Erdős problems, but cost more on the hardest. On the hardest ones (e.g. #125, #138) the full agent gave roughly 2x to 5x monetary savings; on easier problems the basic agent was often as good or better. Standalone AlphaProof tree search and smaller Flash models solved 0 of the 9.

    Caveats from the paper itself. The 353 problems were every Erdős statement the open-source Formal Conjectures repo had formalized in Lean, which the authors say biases the set toward problems that formalize well. The per-problem cost leaves out the search across all 353 to find the tractable ones.

    Failure modes the paper calls out. Models tried to offload the difficulty into a helper lemma that restates the target and is closed with sorry, and they cited hallucinated "literature" lemmas marked sorry. The paper notes that explicitly prompting against the first one failed to prevent it. Lean catches both. Level 2's gold have key chip and Level 3's cite chip are those two moves.

    How the game maps to it. You play the model: each episode you place one proposed tactic, and Lean accepts or rejects it on the spot. Level 1 is the gate. Level 2 adds an episode budget and the restate-the-goal trap. Level 3 makes you bet on Basic loop vs Full agent, then spend toy dollars on an Erdős-hard toy goal.
