🤓 Nerd notes

Level 1 rows are copied from Google DeepMind's Gemini 4 Argon model card (Oct 2026). Single attempt, high thinking. Some Argon scores are Google-run; rival scores come from provider reports or public leaderboards (methodology).

Argon leads 3 of these 6 rows outright and ties 1. It is last of 4 on FrontierSWE v2 and Terminal-bench 4.0. Architecture: sparse mixture-of-experts transformer; 1M token input+output window; inputs text, images, audio, video.

Level 2 follows the Argon for Robotics post (Kanishka Rao). Real2Sim prompt rule "No sim cheating": objects may move only through real MuJoCo contact (mj_step). Banned after t = 0: external forces/torques, welds or equality constraints, setting qpos/qvel, disabling collisions, reporting contact forces not from the solver (e.g. copying logged tactile readings). From a single RGB photo Argon built a MuJoCo twin, a 77-task curriculum and 4,500+ domain-randomized episodes; VLA fine-tuning raised real success 56% → 75% and cut completion time 35 s → 24 s. ERQA (embodied reasoning): 87%.

Toy parts: the cheat events, timing and the "your clean runs → real success" meter are a game mapping (we scale your catch rate onto Google's 56→75 range). They are not Google's method for scoring. The 100-square grids show each benchmark score as squares out of 100.

What's real

Gemini 4 Argon came out Oct 13, 2026. Google's own table shows it is not best at everything. It wins long code fixes (DeepSWE), giant maps of text (GraphWalks) and long videos (LVBench). It comes last of four on command-line jobs (Terminal-bench) and hard frontier coding (FrontierSWE). For robots, Argon builds a sim (a pretend physics world) from one photo, and the rule is: no sim cheating.
