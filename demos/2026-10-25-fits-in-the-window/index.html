Why does the chatbot forget?

The AI can only see so much of a chat. Old messages fall out. Keep the facts it will need.

What you just saw

A chatbot doesn't read words. It reads tokens (word pieces). "Biscuit" is two tokens: "Bisc" and "uit". OpenAI says one token is about ¾ of a word.

The model can only look at a set number of tokens at once. That is its context window (what it can see at once). Your whole chat, plus its own replies, has to fit. When the chat gets too long, many apps cut the oldest messages. So the bot "forgets" what you said first.

Apps fight this with memory: notes they save and paste back in at the top. That works, but those notes use up room too. Keep junk, and good stuff falls out faster. Keep an old fact after it changed, and the AI may use the wrong one.

🤓 Nerd notes

Tokenizer. Every chip is a real token from tiktoken's o200k_base, the encoding GPT-4o uses. We split each line offline and ship the pieces.

Window size. GPT-4o's window is 128,000 tokens (OpenAI docs). The original GPT-3.5-Turbo had about 4,000 (Liu et al., 2023). Ours holds 32 to 48 so you can watch it fill.

What we left out. Real chat formats spend a few hidden tokens per message on role markers. Apps also differ: some cut the oldest turns, some summarize them, some fetch old bits back with search. Our game uses the simplest rule: oldest out first.

Outdated pins. In the game, an old pinned fact always confuses the AI. A real model might pick the newer one, or not. The safe move is the same: don't feed it the stale fact.

Why memory goes at the top. In "Lost in the Middle" (Liu et al., 2023), GPT-3.5-Turbo found the answer 75.8% of the time when it sat first among 20 documents, but 53.8% when it sat in the middle.
