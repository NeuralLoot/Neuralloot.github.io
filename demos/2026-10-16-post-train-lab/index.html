What you just played. In Athanor, four Claude Opus 5.5 agents (in Claude Code), each on its own 8-GPU node, post-train Qwen3-4B-Base for 104 hours with public data, no teacher model and no human research advice. They read scored answers, write their own data and training code, and decide the next experiment. A held-out twin suite they never see gives the final result. You play the team's choices: each cartridge is one recipe step from the winning lineage (Figure 5, Table 11), and the clue on screen is the evidence the agents acted on.

Word map. Base model = pretrained only, continues text · SFT (supervised fine-tuning) = train on worked examples (here 76–87% from one public corpus of reasoning traces, AM-Thinking-v1-Distilled) · RL / GRPO = practice with rule-based rewards · ¼ greedy rollouts = Agent 3 decodes each GRPO rollout greedily with probability ¼, so repetition loops get a negative advantage · Weight merge = average models from different agents · Extrapolate = move to base + f × (merged − base), f 1.2–1.3 · Self-distillation (RFT) = sample 4 answers, keep the shortest correct one, fine-tune on it · Seed = one complete evaluation of a suite · Held-out suite = six twin tasks on different questions, sealed from the agents.

What the paper says it is. Every step is a known method (SFT, RL, merging, self-distillation); the agents chose the composition and wrote the small fixes. The four identical agents converged in the first hour on one corpus and tried few alternatives. One campaign, one 4B base, one agent model; much of the public data was itself distilled from stronger models by its publishers. The team matched the Instruct release, not Qwen's reasoning releases (80.5 / 63.0 for Thinking-2507).

The paper. Zhou, Wang, Fan, Shu, Liu, Zhang, Ling, Wei (ByteDance, Duke, UChicago, Virginia Tech). Athanor: An Agent-Native System for Autonomous Post-Training Research (arXiv 2610.21055).
