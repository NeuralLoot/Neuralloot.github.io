Word map: climber = one LLM agent (Pi agent + DeepSeek V4.1 Flash in the paper) · backpack = session context + candidate history · bubble = lines changed per proposal · 🧠 glow = reasoning tokens per turn · FORGET = discard every session and the search state, restart all agents from the best candidate · flag = target score · $ = model spend.

The paper. Scaling Discovery Requires Forgetting (Tan, Zhou, Agrawal, …, Koushik Sen, Alex Dimakis, Matei Zaharia; UC Berkeley, Stanford, Bespoke Labs; arXiv 2610.20563, Oct 14 2026). LLM agents doing automated research (heuristic programming, kernel optimization, model training) plateau as they pile up history: each agent anchors on its own past tries and makes fewer, smaller edits. Their fix: periodically forget. Wipe every session and the candidate history, restart from the best candidate so far.

How the game maps to it. Level 1: one agent, two forgets. Its edit bubble shrinks as its backpack (history) grows, and gains flatten. Forget when the bubble is tiny and the climb restarts with big edits. Level 2: a team of 4 that shares its best candidate, 3 forgets (the paper's team forgets 3 times = 4 rounds), and stuck turns cost more money. Level 3: decoys. A bubble can dip while the agent is still thinking hard, so you must read both edit size and the 🧠 reasoning glow, like the paper's Figure 5.

Toy parts. Score units, the flag, edit-size curves, the "too soon" heart penalty, and the budget are hand-tuned game rules. The paper forgets on a fixed schedule and says when to forget is still an open question. Forgetting "too soon" costing a heart is our game rule, not a paper result. Research-card numbers come from the paper only.
