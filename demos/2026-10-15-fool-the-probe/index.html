Word map. Activations = the model's internal numbers (its "brain signals") · Readout / probe = a fixed function of the activations, rescaled so the median is 50 and the 1st–99th percentiles are 100 apart ("points") · In-context neurofeedback = the readout is shown back to the model as "Score: n" every turn · Subject S = the model whose activations are read · Writer W = the model that writes the sentence · Control session = identical, but the shown scores are random 0–100 · Objective difference = effect under "maximize" minus effect under "minimize", so drift that ignores the instruction cancels · Monitor = an attention-pooling probe trained to flag deception, truthfulness, and 4 other concepts.

The three tests. Test 1 = Experiment 2 (fixed sentence, prefilled, T = 30): the model writes nothing, so any gap can only come from how it processes the words. Test 2 adds Experiment 1 (free generation, 5 writers × 5 subjects, T = 20): our gray line there is "an outside writer of the same skill", i.e. what the paper's additive fit (writer competence + subject susceptibility, §3.3, Fig. 5) predicts; no diagonal excess was significantly above zero. Test 3 adds Experiments 3–4 (six safety monitors, without and with a chain of thought).

Toy parts. The turn-by-turn wiggles, lives, clocks, and case order are ours, hand-tuned for play. The end values of each pink-minus-gray gap are shaped to the numbers in the table below; we draw the paper's objective difference as a single "pushed the asked way" gap.
