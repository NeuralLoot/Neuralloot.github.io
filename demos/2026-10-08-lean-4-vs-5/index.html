The mechanism

N = v·∇y with v = (√2−1, 1) on the torus 𝕋². Inverting it divides each Fourier mode by 2πi v·k. The paper’s divisor bound (6.7) says |v·k|⁻¹ ≤ C(1+|k|): inverting costs one derivative.

So with m+d derivatives of F, the m-th derivative of N⁻¹F has Fourier terms bounded by about (1+|k|)−(d−1). Over the 2-D lattice that sum converges only if d−1 > 2. That is the paper’s count: four extra derivatives leave a summable (1+|k|)⁻³. Three leave (1+|k|)⁻², which grows like 2π·log R forever.

The Lean theorem norm_derivativeWord_inverse_le (openai/NavierStokesAndEuler @ f9e8bc5) takes another route: a library coefficient bound summed against (1+|k₁|+|k₂|)⁻⁴, with hypotheses on m+5 derivatives. Valid, compiles, and strictly weaker than equation (8.19).

Lean checked that its own statement follows from its own hypotheses. Nothing in that check asks whether the statement is the one printed in the paper.

What this does and doesn’t say

The authors make no claim that OpenAI’s written proof is wrong. If the Lean statement of the main theorem faithfully encodes blow-up, any valid route to it works. The mismatch is about the paper’s intermediate lemmas: the Lean build is not evidence for them. The authors’ point is that an autoformalizer graded only on “compiles” is rewarded for any proof, including one of a different, easier statement.

For scale: OpenAI’s math repo log (Oct 7) reports 300 / 719 top-line results formalized (~42%), plus three manuscripts withdrawn over a sign error and 14 revised.
