What you're playing with

The object. SGF+ (arXiv 2610.10429, Oct 7 2026) trains an autoregressive video model. Each step, the same network denoises the current frames and writes their key-value (KV) context that every future frame attends to. In the predecessor SGF, both jobs update one shared set of weights. The authors measured the two jobs' gradients and found mean directions 104.2° apart in attention and 106.3° in FFN layers, with negative cosine similarity in all 512 prompt–timestep pairs they checked. SGF+ gives each job its own weights (1.4B → 2.8B generator parameters). The paper reports inference on an 81-frame job at 4.969 s vs 4.962 s and a training step about 8.2% slower.

The toy. The weight map is a 2-D stand-in for parameter space. The denoising job wants the weights at the cyan star and the context-writing job wants them at the magenta star. Seen from the starting point, the two pulls are θ apart. Shared weights train on the weighted sum of both pulls, Δw ∝ (1−λ)(a−w) + λ(b−w), and settle on the segment between the stars. Wherever they land, one job is left with error. Split weights put each job on its own star (plus a small floor error e₀ that every model has).

The rollout. Context error compounds, because every frame reads memory written by earlier frames: dD/dt = r·(e_ctx+e₀)·(1+k·D). Denoising error doesn't compound, but it makes every frame noisy right away. A frame stops being watchable when D + β·(e_denoise+e₀) ≥ 1. Constants: r = 0.006, k = 4, β = 0.8, e₀ = 0.05. These are illustrative and were picked by hand. Survival times in the toy are not predictions for any real model.

What the toy shows honestly: once θ is above roughly 90°, most of the summed update cancels out and no shared compromise keeps both errors small. Lean toward denoising and the memory rots, so the video drifts. Lean toward context and every frame comes out noisy from the start. Drop θ and a shared model can make it, because the jobs mostly agree.

So the real effect is mostly on consistency over time, which is what this toy dramatizes. Image quality per frame barely moves, and the motion score drops (the authors argue the baselines' higher scores come from scene jumps and morphing). The paper's 24-hour generations are shown as four qualitative examples in its Appendix D, with no metrics.
