What's real? Paper vs toy parts

The paper. ALONE (Yang, Long, Yu, Zhang, Ge, Wang, Wang; Qiyuan Lab and Tsinghua University; arXiv 2610.11591, submitted Oct 8 2026) is a Bayesian spatial world model for "navigation under intermittent perception". The drone keeps an internal 3D belief of the space around it, moves that belief forward using its own executed actions, and fills in unseen structure with learned priors over common geometry. It also predicts a reliability map, and it asks for a new depth image only when low reliability would change what it does next. The point: a shared camera can do other jobs between looks.

How the game maps to it. The ghost pillars are the drone's belief. Between looks, the belief drifts away from the real obstacles as motion error builds up (level 1). Rows you haven't seen are filled in from the repeating pattern, like the paper's learned priors on its "Lattice" scenes. Level 2's walls copy the paper's "Trap" scenes, where passages hide behind occluders and only a look from close enough can find them. Level 3 drifts faster, a nod to the real flights, where the team requested depth sooner and looked on 7.0% of steps. "Looks per step" counts decision steps at 10 Hz, the paper's decision rate. The free look at takeoff isn't counted.

The pushback, from the paper itself. Both simulated scene families are procedurally generated and static; dynamic scenes are future work. The 0.9% and 1.3% are medians over successful flights only. The policy is also forced to look after at most five observation-free belief updates, to stay inside its training distribution. The real-world test was 10 indoor flights of 10 m each, with a more conservative setting than in simulation.
