From the paper (real numbers)

  • 7,504 rare-disease probands (Genomics England). Cryptic splice variants called by SpliceAI2: 15% of the excess genetic burden, on top of 50% from protein-truncating variants.
  • Half of those splice calls sit >50 bp into introns, typically missed by exome sequencing.
  • "17% more": 133 vs 114 excess variants for the next best model, at a fixed odds ratio of 2.
  • GTEx cryptic-splice benchmark: 4,545 real vs 573,967 null variants. auPRC 0.77 vs 0.66 (SpliceAI).
  • Tissue caveat: reference splicing differences predicted tissue-specific variant effects with no additional benefit from tissue-specific predictions.
Gene, scores & splice model: toy

What's real, what's toy

Real (from the SpliceAI2 manuscript, Jaganathan et al. 2026): the model reads about 200 kb of DNA, predicts splice-site usage and splice-junction usage, and assembles transcripts on a splice graph: candidate sites are nodes, candidate junctions are edges, and each transcript is a path whose usage comes from the sum of node and edge weights. It was trained on 314,745 RNA-seq samples from human and nine other mammals plus 330 long-read samples, and reconstructs the most abundant transcript for 82% of held-out genes (78% without long reads). All numbers in the side panel are quoted from the paper.

Toy (this page): the 3,000-nt gene is synthetic. Splice-site probabilities come from a hand-written motif score (donor MAG|GTRAGT, acceptor = polypyrimidine tract + YAG|G), not from SpliceAI2. Transcripts are the paths through the graph, scored as the sum of node logits; internal exons must be 30 to 300 nt and introns at least 70 nt; usage is a softmax over path scores. The NMD call uses the textbook 50-nt rule. The ISM scan does what the paper's in-silico mutagenesis does in spirit: try every possible single-letter edit and record how much the output moves.

What to try: the dormant acceptor in intron 1 is half-strong (p = 0.50) but has no donor to pair with, so the cell ignores it. Find the one-letter edits downstream that give it a partner. Two of them shift the reading frame and truncate the protein. A third splices in a 117-nt exon that stays in frame. Then try the near-perfect donor in intron 2: its site score jumps to 0.99 and the transcript doesn't change, because nothing can pair with it. That gap between site and transcript is why SpliceAI2 models junctions and whole transcripts, not just sites.
