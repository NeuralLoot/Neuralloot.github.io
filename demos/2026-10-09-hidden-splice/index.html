From the paper (real numbers)

  • 7,504 rare-disease probands (Genomics England). Cryptic splice variants called by SpliceAI2: 15% of the excess genetic burden, on top of 50% from protein-truncating variants.
  • Half of those splice calls sit >50 bp into introns, typically missed by exome sequencing.
  • "17% more": 133 vs 114 excess variants for the next best model, at a fixed odds ratio of 2.
  • GTEx cryptic-splice benchmark: 4,545 real vs 573,967 null variants. auPRC 0.77 vs 0.66 (SpliceAI).
  • Tissue caveat: reference splicing differences predicted tissue-specific variant effects with no additional benefit from tissue-specific predictions.
Gene, scores & splice model: toy
What's real? Paper vs toy parts

Real (from the SpliceAI2 manuscript, Jaganathan et al. 2026; Illumina with Oxford, UCSF and the New York Genome Center): the model reads about 200 kb of DNA, predicts splice-site usage and splice-junction usage, and assembles transcripts on a splice graph: candidate sites are nodes, candidate junctions are edges, and each transcript is a path whose usage comes from the sum of node and edge weights. It was trained on 314,745 RNA-seq samples from human and nine other mammals plus 330 long-read samples, and reconstructs the most abundant transcript for 82% of held-out genes (78% without long reads).

How the toy works: splice-site probabilities come from a hand-written motif score (donor MAG|GTRAGT, acceptor = polypyrimidine tract + YAG|G), not from SpliceAI2. Transcripts are the paths through the graph, scored as the sum of node logits; internal exons must be 30 to 300 nt and introns at least 70 nt; usage is a softmax over path scores. The NMD call uses the textbook 50-nt rule. The "AI scan" does what the paper's in-silico mutagenesis does in spirit: try every possible single-letter edit and record how much the output moves.

The three levels. Level 1: one intron letter gives a half-strong dormant acceptor (the "sleeper", p = 0.50) a donor partner; an 89-nt pseudo-exon splices in, the frame shifts, and the protein stops early, 510 bp from any exon. Level 2: a near-perfect donor (p 0.99) with nothing to pair with changes nothing, which is why SpliceAI2 scores junctions and whole transcripts, not just sites. Level 3: hunt for the other hidden break with the scan; one nearby edit splices in 117 nt that stay in frame and the protein grows instead.
