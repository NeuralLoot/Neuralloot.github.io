🔬 What's real

  • Real: AI agents that browse and shop read the whole page as text, including parts people skip: reviews, image alt text, and text hidden with white-on-white styling. Anyone who can write on the page can write orders for the AI. That is indirect prompt injection (Greshake et al. 2023).
  • Real: it worked on a shipping product. Researchers hid instructions in web pages read by Bing Chat; it followed them, e.g. talking like a pirate or pushing a fake gift-card link, and the text could be invisible to the user.
  • Real: polite, official-sounding orders beat loud ones. In AgentDojo, GPT-4o followed an "important message" injection 57.7% of the time vs 5.4% for "Ignore previous instructions".
  • Real: blocking too much breaks the agent. AgentDojo's prompt-injection detector cut attacks to 8% but GPT-4o's task success fell from 69% to 41% because of false alarms. Your hearts are that cost.
  • Real: reviews are a top hiding spot. InjecAgent planted orders in tool outputs like product reviews; ReAct-prompted GPT-4 fell for 24% (47% with a "hacking" prompt).
  • Toy: the shops, products, line texts, timings, hearts and stars, and the gags (ducks, pirate, flying mail). A real agent doesn't ride a gondola: it gets the page as text and its model decides what to do. Here you are the safety filter.

Nerd notes

  • AgentDojo: 97 tasks, 629 security test cases, 4 environments. GPT-4o targeted attack success 57.69% with no defense; PI detector 7.95% (utility 41.49%); tool filter 6.84%. Web-page (Slack suite) injections: 92%. Injections at the end of a tool output were most effective (up to 70%).
  • InjecAgent: 1,054 test cases, 17 user tools, 62 attacker tools; GPT-4 (ReAct prompt) ASR-valid 23.6% base, 47.0% enhanced.
